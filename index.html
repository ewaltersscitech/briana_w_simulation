Following Gleason

Gleason Che Stop light at Sunset & Vine, 90 m from start
The problem

My parents and I are visiting our family friend Gleason and his wife and three kids in LA. Gleason wants to go to a restaurant that for some odd reason is not on Google Maps. Since my parents and I don’t know our way around very well, Gleason proposes that we just follow their car to the spot. In agreement, we all get into our cars starting at the same position on Sunset Boulevard. Gleason accelerates 3.0 m/s² until he reaches a velocity of 18 m/s, then holds that speed. My dad, Che, begins driving 1.0 s after Gleason does, accelerating 2.0 m/s² until he reaches 12 m/s, then holds that speed. Gleason makes it through the stop light at Sunset and Vine, which is 90 m away from the starting point, but it turns red before Che gets there. Che, realizing he’s not going to make the light, begins braking at 18 m before the light and stops exactly at it. The light turns green 7.0 s later. Not wanting to lose Gleason, Che accelerates 4.5 m/s² until he reaches 27 m/s, then holds that speed.

  1. Write Gleason's position equation.
  2. Write Che’s position equation.
  3. At what time does Che come to a stop at the light, and how far ahead is Gleason at that moment?
  4. What are Gleason and Che’s velocities at the instant Che stops at the light?
  5. At what time and position does Che catch up to Gleason?
  6. When the light turns green and Che starts speeding up, does the gap between him and Gleason start shrinking right away? Explain.

Worked solution

t is measured from the moment Gleason starts. Each stage has constant acceleration, so every step uses v = v₀ + at, Δx = v₀t + ½at², or v² = v₀² + 2aΔx. Answers are given to 3 significant figures.

(a) Gleason’s position equation

Equationv = v₀ + at → t = (v − v₀) / a
Substitutet = (18 − 0) / 3.0 = 6.00 s
EquationΔx = v₀t + ½at²
SubstituteΔx = 0 + ½(3.0)(6.00)² = 54.0 m
0 ≤ t ≤ 6.00 sx = 1.50t²
t > 6.00 sx = 54.0 + 18.0(t − 6.00)

(b) Che’s position equation

Speeding up (starts at t = 1.00 s)

Substitutet = (12 − 0) / 2.0 = 6.00 s → ends at t = 7.00 s
SubstituteΔx = ½(2.0)(6.00)² = 36.0 m

Braking (starts 18 m before the light, at x = 72.0 m)

Equationv² = v₀² + 2aΔx → a = (v² − v₀²) / (2Δx)
Substitutea = (0² − 12²) / (2 × 18) = −4.00 m/s²
Substitutet = (0 − 12) / (−4.00) = 3.00 s

Holding speed between 36.0 m and 72.0 m

Substitutet = Δx / v = (72.0 − 36.0) / 12 = 3.00 s → braking runs t = 10.0 s to 13.0 s

After the green light (t = 13.0 + 7.0 = 20.0 s)

Substitutet = (27 − 0) / 4.5 = 6.00 s → ends at t = 26.0 s
SubstituteΔx = ½(4.5)(6.00)² = 81.0 m → x = 90.0 + 81.0 = 171 m
0 ≤ t < 1.00 sx = 0
1.00 ≤ t ≤ 7.00 sx = 1.00(t − 1.00)²
7.00 < t ≤ 10.0 sx = 36.0 + 12.0(t − 7.00)
10.0 < t ≤ 13.0 sx = 72.0 + 12.0(t − 10.0) − 2.00(t − 10.0)²
13.0 < t ≤ 20.0 sx = 90.0
20.0 < t ≤ 26.0 sx = 90.0 + 2.25(t − 20.0)²
t > 26.0 sx = 171 + 27.0(t − 26.0)

(c) When Che stops, and Gleason’s lead

From (b)Che stops at t = 10.0 + 3.00 = 13.0 s
Equationx_G = 54.0 + 18.0(t − 6.00)
Substitutex_G = 54.0 + 18.0(13.0 − 6.00) = 180 m; gap = 180 − 90.0 = 90.0 m
t = 13.0 s; Gleason is 90.0 m ahead

(d) Velocities when Che stops

Gleasont = 13.0 s > 6.00 s, so he is holding v = 18.0 m/s
Chev = v₀ + at = 12 + (−4.00)(3.00) = 0.00 m/s
Gleason: 18.0 m/s; Che: 0.00 m/s

(e) When and where Che catches Gleason

At t = 26.0 s, Gleason is at 54.0 + 18.0(20.0) = 414 m and Che is at 171 m, so Che is still behind. Set the two constant-speed equations equal.

Equationx_G = x_C
Substitute54.0 + 18.0(t − 6.00) = 171 + 27.0(t − 26.0)
Solve18.0t − 54.0 = 27.0t − 531 → 9.00t = 477 → t = 53.0 s
Positionx = 54.0 + 18.0(53.0 − 6.00) = 900 m
t = 53.0 s; x = 9.00 × 10² m from the start

(f) Does the gap shrink right away?

No. The gap shrinks only when the car behind is faster than the car ahead. Right after the light turns green, Che is going slower than Gleason’s 18 m/s, so Gleason keeps pulling away. The gap is largest at the instant their speeds are equal, and it starts closing only once Che is faster than 18 m/s.
